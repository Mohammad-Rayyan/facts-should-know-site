Wombats are the only animals that produce cube-shaped poop

Published November 25, 2026

Wombats deposit 80 to 100 cubic pellets of feces per day, and they stack them on rocks and logs. The cubes mark territory. They take their shape in the final stretch of the intestine, where the walls are not evenly stiff.

A daily stack on rocks and logs

Wombats leave their droppings in plain view, and they leave a lot of them. The count is 80 to 100 cubic pellets per day. The pellets are stacked on rocks and logs, and the stacks mark territory.

Rocks and logs are uneven surfaces. A round dropping would tend to roll away. A cube stays where it is put, which makes the feces more effective as territorial markers on that kind of ground.

How the cube forms

Most mammals produce cylindrical or formless feces, shaped by the circular muscles of the intestines. Wombats are the only known exception.

Work published in 2021 identified that the final 8 percent of the wombat intestine has regions of varying stiffness. As the feces are compressed, those regions shape them into cubes. The result is a distinctly cubic dropping, and it puzzled scientists for years.

Three species, one shape

The trait is not limited to one kind of wombat. The common wombat, the southern hairy-nosed wombat and the northern hairy-nosed wombat all produce cube-shaped feces. No wombat species is left out.

What is still open

Wombats are the only known animals to produce cube-shaped feces. Whether they are the only ones at all is hard to verify across every animal species. No other examples are documented in scientific literature, which is not the same as proof that none exist.

The 8 percent figure and the precise mechanical process are also still being refined through ongoing research.
