Space Shuttle Discovery flew 39 missions, more than any other orbiter

Published November 28, 2026

Space Shuttle Discovery flew 39 orbital missions between 1984 and 2011. No other shuttle orbiter flew as many. NASA's fact sheet lists about 365 days in space and 5,830 orbits of Earth.

From August 1984 to March 2011

Discovery's first flight was STS-41-D, which launched on August 30, 1984. Its 39th and last was STS-133, which launched on February 24, 2011 and landed on March 9, 2011.

Between those two flights, the orbiter logged the 5,830 orbits that NASA's fact sheet lists.

How the rest of the fleet compares

The other orbiters flew fewer missions. Atlantis flew 33, Columbia 28, Endeavour 25 and Challenger 10. The shuttle program as a whole totaled 135 missions.

A few limits apply to the numbers. The count of 39 covers orbital missions only. It does not include the approach and landing tests flown by Enterprise. The days-in-space figure is rounded, and sources give a little over 365 days, so about 365 is the safer way to put it. And the claim of more flights than any other orbiter holds for the shuttle fleet only. It says nothing about other spacecraft.

Two returns to flight and one telescope

Discovery flew both of the shuttle program's return-to-flight missions. STS-26 launched in September 1988, after the Challenger accident. STS-114 launched in July 2005, after the Columbia accident.

In April 1990, on STS-31, Discovery launched the Hubble Space Telescope.

After the last landing

Discovery was retired after STS-133. It was later moved to the Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum.

Discovery flew 39 orbital missions, more than any other shuttle orbiter, and spent about 365 days in space doing it.
