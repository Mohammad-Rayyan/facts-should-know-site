Booksellers took up Darwin's 1,250-copy first printing of Origin of Species in 1859

Published November 23, 2026

John Murray printed 1,250 copies of On the Origin of Species. Booksellers were reported to have taken the lot at a trade sale held about 22 November 1859, a couple of days before the book was published on 24 November. The popular line is that it sold out on day one, which is close but not quite right.

A modest first printing

On the Origin of Species was published by John Murray in London on 24 November 1859. The first printing ran to 1,250 copies. Darwin had worked on his theory of natural selection for about two decades by then, and Murray chose a modest print run.

The book was priced at 15 shillings at publication.

The trade sale

The buyers were not readers queuing at a shop. Booksellers placed orders at Murray's trade sale, held a couple of days before the official publication date, about 22 November 1859. Those orders were reported to have exhausted the stock before publication day.

So the accurate version is that the book trade took up the printing. It was not 1,250 individual readers buying on one day.

What the simple story leaves out

Not every copy was sold at retail. Some copies of the printing went to reviewers and as presentation copies.

Accounts also differ slightly on exact sale figures and dates. The report that all the copies were taken up comes from contemporary correspondence and later histories, not from a single formal sales record that can be cited precisely.

A second edition

Demand was strong, and Murray brought out a second edition of 3,000 copies in January 1860. The first printing had been 1,250.

Booksellers reportedly took up all 1,250 copies of the first printing at the trade sale before publication on 24 November 1859. The 'sold out on day one' story holds up only if you say who the buyers were.
