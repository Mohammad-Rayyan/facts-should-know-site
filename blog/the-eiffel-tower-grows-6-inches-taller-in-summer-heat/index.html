The Eiffel Tower grows 6 inches taller in summer heat

Published November 23, 2026

On a hot summer day, the Eiffel Tower is taller than it is in winter. The operator of the tower says the iron structure can grow by up to about 15 centimeters, or about 6 inches. The change is real, but it is a ceiling, not a daily event.

An iron tower that moves with the weather

The Eiffel Tower is made of puddled wrought iron. It was built between 1887 and 1889 for the Exposition Universelle in Paris, and it stands about 330 metres tall including antennas.

The Société d'Exploitation de la Tour Eiffel, which runs the tower, says it can grow by up to about 15 cm in summer heat.

Where the 15 centimeters comes from

Wrought iron has a coefficient of linear thermal expansion of roughly 11 to 12 millionths per degree Celsius. That is the amount a length of the metal grows for each degree of warming, as a fraction of its size.

Apply that to a 300 m iron structure that warms by about 40 °C. It would lengthen by roughly 13 to 15 cm. The arithmetic lands close to the figure the operator gives.

What the number does not tell you

The 15 cm figure is an upper bound. It depends on the temperature range, and the real change is usually smaller. The exact number also rests on assumptions about how much of the structure actually heats up.

The sources that give the figure are mostly the tower's operator and popular references, so it is best read as a good estimate, not a precise measurement.

Sideways movement and the return to size

The sunlit side of the metal heats more than the shaded side. Because of that, the top can also move sideways by several centimeters.

The growth is temporary. As temperatures fall in cooler weather, the tower shrinks again.

The Eiffel Tower can grow by up to about 15 cm in summer heat, then shrinks back as the weather cools. Treat 15 cm as the upper limit, not the usual change.
